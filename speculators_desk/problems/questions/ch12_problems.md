### Problem 12.1 (Level 1) — Diagnosis
For each situation, say whether the owner faces a liquidity problem, a solvency problem, or neither, and whether the demand loss (if any) looks cyclical or structural. Say which additional fact would change your answer.

(a) A 95%-leased industrial building's 3.25% interest-only loan matures. At today's rates the maximum new loan is \$6 million below the balance. The building is worth 40% more than the loan.
(b) A suburban office building's two largest tenants renewed at 55% of their previous square footage. Their headcount in the region is unchanged.
(c) A hotel's RevPAR fell 30% in a recession. Its loan has three years to maturity and a debt-service reserve covering 18 months.
(d) A garden apartment property in a metro where deliveries over the past two years equal 14% of stock. Occupancy has fallen from 95% to 86%, and the floating-rate loan's interest-rate cap expires in four months. The property is worth about the loan balance.

### Problem 12.2 (Level 2) — The rate reset
A property earns NOI of \$2.0 million. Its \$25.0 million loan is interest-only at 4.00%.
(a) What is the DSCR?
(b) What is the DSCR on the same balance if the rate resets to 6.50% interest-only?
(c) What is it if the new loan must amortize over 30 years at 6.50% (monthly payments)? Give the loan constant.
Do (a) and (b) without a calculator.

### Problem 12.3 (Level 2) — Texas notice and the concentration screens
(a) A trustee gives notice of a Texas foreclosure sale on Thursday 14 January 2027. Using the rule in Example 12.4 (sale on the first Tuesday of a month; notice at least 21 days before), what is the earliest sale date, and how many days after notice is it?
(b) A bank has total risk-based capital of \$80 million, CLD loans of \$70 million, and total non-owner-occupied CRE loans of \$250 million, up from \$150 million 36 months ago. Which of the 2006 interagency screens does it meet?

### Problem 12.4 (Level 3) — Stock-flow by hand
A market has stock 50.0 million sf, latent demand 46.0 million sf, rent \$20.00, natural vacancy 8%, rent response $\alpha = 1.0$, feasibility rent \$20.00, starts response $\kappa = 0.5$, a two-year construction lag, and no depreciation. Space under construction before year 1 will complete 0 million sf in year 1 and 1.0 million sf in year 2. Demand grows 4% in years 1 and 2 and 0% in year 3.
(a) Compute stock, vacancy, rent, completions, and starts for years 1–3, using the equations of §12.4.1.
(b) Demand stops growing in year 3. What happens to starts in year 3, and why? What does this imply for vacancy in years 4 and 5?

### Problem 12.5 (Level 3) — Pricing a small NPL
A \$20.0 million UPB loan. Scenarios:

| Scenario | $p$ | Gross recovery | Costs | Years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cure (payoff with interest) | 0.15 | \$21,000,000 | \$100,000 | 1.0 |
| Discounted payoff | 0.25 | \$16,500,000 | \$100,000 | 0.5 |
| Foreclose and resell | 0.45 | \$15,000,000 | \$1,200,000 | 1.5 |
| Bankruptcy, then resale | 0.15 | \$14,000,000 | \$2,000,000 | 3.0 |

(a) Maximum bid at a 15% required return.
(b) At a \$13.0 million bid, the annualized return in each scenario and the expected NPV at 15%.
(c) Explain in two sentences why you should not quote the probability-weighted average of the scenario returns as "the expected return".

### Problem 12.6 (Level 3) — Appraisal reduction
A \$30.0 million CMBS loan with a 4.50% coupon is in special servicing. Accrued unpaid interest \$0.9 million; unreimbursed advances \$0.3 million; other amounts due (taxes and fees) \$0.2 million; reserves held \$0.1 million. New appraisal \$20.0 million. Use the generic definition in §12.4.5 with a 10% haircut.
(a) Compute the ARA.
(b) Compute the reduced monthly interest advance.
(c) The controlling class has an original balance of \$20.0 million. Using a 25% control threshold after ARA, does control shift? What appraisal value would make it shift exactly?

### Problem 12.7 (Level 3) — REIT signals
A REIT trades at \$18.00 with 250 million shares. Debt \$6.0 billion, no preferred, cash \$0.2 billion, other non-real-estate assets \$0.5 billion. Forward cash NOI \$720 million. Private appraisals imply a 6.25% cap rate.
(a) The implied cap rate.
(b) NAV per share and the discount.
(c) Your colleague says private values must fall 28%. Correct the statement: by how much must the private cap rate rise, and by how much must gross asset value fall, for NAV to equal the share price?

### Problem 12.8 (Level 4) — The bank's choice
A bank has total risk-based capital of \$120 million and non-owner-occupied CRE loans of \$420 million. The board wants CRE/capital at or below 300% within two quarters. Tax rate 21%.
(a) Compute the par amount of loans it must sell if the best bids are 85, 70, and 65 cents on the dollar.
(b) How much new capital achieves the same ratio with no sales?
(c) Recommend a course of action. As a loan buyer, what does the bank's arithmetic tell you about which loans it will offer you?

### Problem 12.9 (Level 4) — The incumbent's dilemma
You own a 250,000 sf office building bought at \$95 million with a \$70 million interest-only loan at 6.00%. Operating expenses are \$2.75 million per year and recurring leasing capital \$0.875 million per year. Stabilized occupancy is 85%. Market gross rent is \$40.00/sf. Across the street, a buyer has just acquired an identical building out of foreclosure at a \$40 million all-in basis and targets a 10% yield.
(a) The rent each owner needs: you at 6.5% on your basis, the new owner at 10%; and the rent at which your NOI exactly covers debt service.
(b) Your DSCR and yield on basis at \$40.00. Then at \$36.00 if the new owner sets market rent there.
(c) What do you do? Consider cutting rent, holding rent and losing occupancy, a loan modification, and selling. Rubric in the solutions.

### Problem 12.10 (Level 5) — Five days to bid
A special servicer's broker invites you to bid on a defaulted \$62 million CMBS loan secured by a 1990s suburban office campus in a judicial-foreclosure state. Bids are due in five business days. You receive:
* An appraisal 18 months old at \$58 million, prepared when the property was 81% leased. Current occupancy is "approximately 50%" according to the broker. No current rent roll.
* A note that the largest tenant (30% of the space) "has given notice", with no date or lease document.
* The trust's controlling-class holder is an affiliate of the special servicer and has the right to match any bid.
* The borrower's sponsor has filed a lawsuit against the servicer alleging improper default.
* Loan documents are in the data room; the guaranty is not.

Write a one-page memo: bid, pass, or ask for time. Address (a) the value range you can defend with this information, (b) the effect of the right to match on your expected return, (c) the litigation and judicial timeline, (d) what you would need to see before bidding, and (e) whether abstention is the best answer.
