### Solution 11.1
(a) Any three of: (i) **the lease is one night**: hotel revenue reprices daily, while office income is fixed by leases until they roll; (ii) **operating leverage**: a large share of hotel costs (fixed labor, A&G, S&M, POM, utilities base load, tax, insurance) does not fall with revenue, so NOI falls by a multiple of the revenue decline (DOL of about 4 in Example 11.2); (iii) **revenue-based fees are senior**: franchise and base management fees fall only in proportion to revenue, so they take a rising share of a shrinking GOP; (iv) **ADR and occupancy fall together** in downturns, so revenue losses come with discounting as well as volume; (v) the office building's tenants carry the operating business risk; the hotel owner carries it directly.

(b) Not inconsistent if both describe the same price. A multiple of 11× EBITDA is a 9.09% "cap rate" on EBITDA. With the reserve at 4% of revenue, $c_{NOI} = c_{EBITDA} \times NOI/EBITDA$. For example, with EBITDA at 23% and NOI at 19% of revenue, $c_{NOI} = 9.09\% \times 19/23 = 7.5\%$. The two quotes are consistent only if the user knows which line each refers to. The error is to take a cap rate quoted on one line and apply it to the other (Example 11.3).

(c) RGI strips out market movements by dividing by the comp set's RevPAR. RevPAR growth mixes the market tide with the hotel's own performance (Example 11.1). RGI can mislead if the comp set is stale or chosen to flatter (a set of weaker hotels), if a comp hotel was closed or renovating during the period, or if the subject's room count changed. A level above 100 is less informative than the trend.

(d) The **FF&E reserve** is a recurring set-aside (here 4% of revenue) for routine replacement; lenders escrow it and buyers deduct it in NOI. A **PIP** is a brand-mandated renovation triggered by relicensing or sale; buyers deduct it from price (equation 11.3) and lenders hold PIP reserves. **Deferred capital** (roofs, parking, elevators, structural or MEP items outside brand standards) is identified in a property condition assessment; it is deducted from price by a careful buyer but captured by neither the reserve nor the PIP letter. Case D separates the three.

### Solution 11.3
MPI $= 100 \times 66/71 = 93.0$; ARI $= 100 \times 172/160 = 107.5$; RGI $= 100 \times (0.66 \times 172)/(0.71 \times 160) = 113.52/113.60 \times 100 = 99.9$.
The hotel is pricing 7.5% above the comp set and giving up 7 points of occupancy share to do it. RGI of 99.9 says the rate premium has almost exactly paid for the lost occupancy in revenue. In profit it has done better than that, because rate-led revenue flows through at a higher rate than occupancy-led revenue (Example 11.2). Whether the strategy "works" depends on contribution, not RGI: with \$35–40 of variable cost per occupied room, fewer rooms at a higher rate produce more profit at the same RevPAR.

### Solution 11.5
(a) Contribution $= 120 \times 1.02 \times (1 - 0.20) - 28 = 97.92 - 28 = \$69.92$.
(b) Rooms available $= 36{,}500$. Before debt: $1{,}250{,}000/69.92 = 17{,}878$ room-nights, **48.98%**. After debt: $(1{,}250{,}000 + 550{,}000)/69.92 = 25{,}744$ room-nights, **70.53%**.
(c) ADR \$110.40: contribution $= 110.40 \times 1.02 \times 0.80 - 28 = \$62.09$; break-even $= 1{,}800{,}000/62.09/36{,}500 = $ **79.43%**.
(d) An 8% ADR decline raises break-even occupancy by about 9 points. A stress test that cuts occupancy but holds ADR understates the stress; in both 2001 and 2020 ADR fell with occupancy (§11.10). A lender should test break-even at a stressed ADR, and should note that a hotel needing 79% occupancy to cover debt service in a downturn has almost no cushion.

### Solution 11.7

(b) ACF $= 11{,}500{,}000 - 4{,}200{,}000 = \$7{,}300{,}000$. Incentive fee $= 0.15 \times (7{,}300{,}000 - 6{,}300{,}000) = \$150{,}000$. Owner cash \$7,150,000.
(c) Incentive fee $= 0.08 \times 9{,}600{,}000 = \$768{,}000$ and $0.08 \times 11{,}500{,}000 = \$920{,}000$. Owner cash \$4,632,000 and \$6,380,000.
(d) The priority-based contract. The GOP-based fee costs the owner \$768,000 in a year when the owner earns 7.7% on capital (5.4/70) and \$770,000 more in the good year. ### Solution 11.10 (Level 5) — rubric problem
(a) The gap in rooms revenue is $(104 - 92) \times 100 \times 365 = \$438{,}000$. At 75% flow-through that is \$328,500 of NOI and, at 9.5%, **\$3,457,895** of value: probably a third or more of the hotel's value. A point estimate that picks one figure without resolving the gap is a guess.

(b) Explanations and the document that resolves each:

| Explanation | Resolving document |
|---|---|
| T-12 rooms revenue includes non-room items (resort or parking fees, pet fees, no-show and cancellation revenue, F&B package allocations) | General ledger detail for the rooms revenue account |
| T-12 includes occupancy taxes | Tax filings (state and local occupancy tax returns) reconciled to revenue |
| Different periods or room counts (rooms out of order excluded from availability) | STR participation records; the hotel's room-count history |
| STR submission errors or late corrections | STR data submission history; daily reports from the PMS |
| Borrower overstatement | Bank deposits; occupancy-tax filings; credit-card processor statements |

(c) **Model response.** "We will deliver tomorrow a conditional value range on each RevPAR basis, a list of the documents needed to reconcile the two, and our estimate of PIP and tax exposure. We will not deliver a point value until the rooms revenue is reconciled to occupancy-tax filings and the brand states its renewal requirements, because those two facts move value by more than any cap-rate judgment." A point value is not the right output.

**Rubric (10 points).**

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 2 | Quantifies the discrepancy's value effect (about \$3.5 million at the stated assumptions) |
| 2 | Lists at least three explanations and a document that resolves each; names occupancy-tax filings or bank deposits as the independent check |
| 2 | Identifies the franchise expiry and missing PIP letter as a second, separate value driver; notes the reassessment issue |
| 2 | Declines to give a point value and explains why, while still delivering something useful (a conditional range and a diligence list) |
| 2 | Professional tone toward the servicer: states what would allow a firm value and when |

Deductions: −4 for a firm point value at either RevPAR; −2 for treating the STR figure as automatically correct without explanation (STR data can also be wrong). **Abstention from a point value is the best answer.**

### Decision exercise (Chapter 11 §11.11) — model solution and rubric

**Model answers.**

1. **Value.** Stabilized, unencumbered value at a 9% cap on NOI after a 4% reserve: \$13.66 million. As-is with a \$14,000/key PIP priced at a 10% contingency and 15% required profit: **\$11.17 million**. A range of about \$10.4–12.0 million is defensible pending the PIP letter: \$10.38 million with a \$16,500/key PIP at a 9.25% cap, and \$11.98 million with an \$11,500/key PIP at 8.75%. The most probable price is near \$11.2 million.
2. **The owner's number.** "\$1.46 million is EBITDA, before the 4% FF&E reserve that every buyer and lender deducts. On NOI the 9% cap gives \$13.7 million; the difference, \$2.53 million, is the reserve capitalized. A buyer must also fund the brand's renovation, about \$2.0 million with contingency, and will expect a return for doing so. Those two facts, not the market, separate your number from a buyer's."
3. **Share.** RGI fell from 105.0 to 102.8 while the market grew 9.4%. Restoring the prior share is worth \$92,647 of NOI and about \$1.03 million of value, but a buyer will pay for it only in part, because the buyer must earn it, and the cause (rate overreach or product decline) is unknown. The PIP may be the remedy if the cause is the product.
4. **Timing.** Obtain the PIP letter first. Then apply Case 11.8: compare the PIP-adjusted sale price with the owner's renovate-and-sell value under exit-cap and overrun scenarios. Selling now is better unless the owner has the liquidity and appetite for renovation and cap-rate risk.
5. **Abstention.** Decline to publish a point value until the PIP letter arrives; publish a conditional range now.

**Rubric (20 points).**

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 4 | EBITDA/NOI error identified and quantified (\$2.53 million = capitalized reserve) |
| 4 | PIP deducted with contingency, displacement, carry and required profit (equation 11.3) |
| 4 | RGI decline identified as a diligence issue, with the value of restoring share quantified and discounted |
| 4 | Range rather than a point, with the width justified by named uncertainties |
| 4 | Recommendation conditioned on the PIP letter; timing analysis or explicit plan to do it |

Fails: publishes \$16.2 million, or any as-is figure above \$13.7 million without a PIP letter.
